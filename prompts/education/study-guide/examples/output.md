# Example Output: Active Study Guide Generator

## Input Values Used
- **Subject/Topic:** Photosynthesis
- **Target Grade Level:** High School (9th/10th Grade)
- **Source Material/Notes:** Photosynthesis happens in plants. Light reactions take place in the thylakoid membrane using sunlight and water to make ATP and NADPH, releasing oxygen. Dark reactions (Calvin Cycle) happen in the stroma using CO2, ATP, and NADPH to make glucose.

---

### Core Concepts
- **Energy Conversion:** Photosynthesis converts light energy into stored chemical energy (glucose) inside plant cells.
- **Two Main Stages:**
  - **Light-Dependent Reactions:** Occur in the thylakoid membranes; require sunlight and water to produce oxygen, ATP, and NADPH.
  - **Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle):** Occur in the stroma; use carbon dioxide, ATP, and NADPH to produce glucose.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Chloroplast:** The plant cell organelle where photosynthesis takes place.
- **Thylakoid:** Membrane-bound structures inside chloroplasts where light-dependent reactions occur.
- **Stroma:** The fluid-filled space surrounding thylakoids inside the chloroplast where the Calvin Cycle occurs.
- **ATP & NADPH:** Energy-carrying molecules that store power generated by light reactions to fuel glucose creation.

### Active Recall Self-Test
1. **Where do the light-dependent reactions take place?**
   - *Answer: In the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast.*
2. **What are the primary outputs of the Calvin Cycle?**
   - *Answer: Glucose (sugar), along with ADP and NADP+ that return to the light reactions.*
3. **What molecule is split during light-dependent reactions to release oxygen?**
   - *Answer: Water (H₂O).*
4. **What is the fluid-filled interior of the chloroplast called?**
   - *Answer: The stroma.*
5. **Why are ATP and NADPH essential for the Calvin Cycle?**
   - *Answer: They provide the chemical energy and electrons needed to convert carbon dioxide into glucose.*
